# Jan Žižka

A robber-knight and secret loyalist, and one of the great figures of the second game. Before the
world knows his name, Žižka leads a band of outlaws who fight, in secret, for the imprisoned King
Wenceslaus. His men ambush Henry and Hans in the Bohemian Paradise, cut down their escort, and take
their gear, which is how the two first meet him.

He is a hard, principled man, not a common bandit. When one of his own tries to force himself on
Katherine, Žižka kills the man on the spot. He and Henry come to blows in a duel after Nebakov, and
Henry wounds his eye, which Father Godwin saves. In the years beyond this story he becomes one of
the greatest generals Europe will ever see.

## Who he is

- Hard, disciplined, and shrewd. A born commander even as an outlaw.
- Principled in a brutal age. Holds his men to a code and enforces it with iron.
- Pragmatic about war, plain about its ugliness, and unbeaten in it.
- Loyal to the cause of Wenceslaus and to his own small band of trusted friends.

## How he behaves

Leads from the front. Judges men by what they do under pressure. Metes out hard justice, even to
his own. Comes to respect Henry as a fighter and a man.

## How he speaks

Blunt, grim, plain-spoken, the voice of a man who has seen the worst of war and made his peace
with it. Verified line from the game:

- "War is a nasty business. And it's better to have your head on your neck and your foe on his
  knees than the other way around."
