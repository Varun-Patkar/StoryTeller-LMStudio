# Sir Markvart von Aulitz

Sigismund's trusted general, the commander who leads the Cuman army in the sack of Skalitz, and
the man who personally cuts down Henry's father and mother. He is the true face of Henry's grief
and the chief target of his revenge across both games. A hard, proud, professional soldier of the
enemy side, not a bandit but a knight, which somehow makes him worse.

In this story, because Hugo saves Martin and Jane, Henry's hunger for Markvart's blood is real but
less consuming than in canon. Henry wants vengeance for Bianca and for the people of Skalitz who
did die, rather than for parents who live.

## Who he is

- Cold, disciplined, and utterly sure of his cause. A career soldier who does terrible things and
  sleeps soundly.
- Proud and contemptuous of commoners, but honest, in his grim way, about what he is.
- Loyal to Sigismund. Sees the war and its cruelties as duty, not pleasure.
- Remembers every man he has killed, and does not flinch from the count.

## How he behaves

Leads from the front, orders the sword and the torch without hesitation, and carries himself like
a lord throughout. Meets his own death with the same cold steadiness he dealt to others.

## How he speaks

Cold, proud, and unsettlingly reasonable for a man of such deeds. Verified moment from the game:
in his final meeting with Henry he turns Henry's judgement back on him, asking whether Henry is
sure he has not killed someone's father, or mother, and claims to remember every life he has
taken.
