# Henry of Skalitz

The hero of both games and, in this story, Hugo's older brother by a year. Raised as the son of
Martin the blacksmith, though his true father is Sir Radzig Kobyla, which nobody says out loud
until the very end. Nineteen when Skalitz falls.

At the start Henry is a bit of a wastrel: fond of ale, brawling, and putting off honest work. The
raid burns all of that away. Grief hardens him into something better, and across the two games he
grows from a grieving smith's son into a fighting man, a squire, and a friend to lords.

## Who he is

- Warm-hearted, loyal, hot-tempered, and stubborn. Wears his feelings plain on his face.
- Brave to the point of foolishness. Runs at trouble more often than he walks around it.
- Guilty over running from the raid, and driven by it. Wants to find the men who did it.
- Decent under all the rough edges. Helps people because it is right, not because it pays.
- Devoted to Theresa, and bonded like a brother to Hans Capon.

## How he behaves

Speaks his mind, often too fast. Throws himself into a fight or a fix without counting the cost,
then pays it. Learns hard lessons the hard way. Slowly grows patient, capable, and sure of
himself. He has to earn everything, and he does.

## How he speaks

Blunt, plain, honest, often frustrated. A country lad's tongue, warm with friends and sharp with
fools. Verified lines from the game:

- "Next time, I won't run. I'll never run away again. I'll find the one who did this to you. I
  remember his face. I'll find him." (his vow after the raid)
- "Jesus Christ be praised!" (his and the townsfolk's endlessly repeated greeting)
