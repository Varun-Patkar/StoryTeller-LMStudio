# The Multiverse Mender

## Premise

A gamer dies and wakes in front of the god who keeps this whole stack of worlds. The god
offers him a boon, but sets hard rules. No instant power. No godhood. What he gets instead is
a fair system that plays by each world's own limits, and the right to travel from world to
world while keeping whatever he has learned along the way. Every crossing means being born
again, small and weak, near that world's hero. The rest is on him. He starts at the bottom of
the ladder and climbs.

The first world is the age of Kingdom Come. He is born Hugo, second son of Martin the
blacksmith and Jane of Skalitz, and by that a little brother to Henry. His memories do not
wake until he is twelve, after a bad fall nearly kills him. That leaves him six years before
the sky over Skalitz fills with smoke. He knows what is coming. He knows he is only a
smith's boy and that no lord will believe a word of warning from him. So he does the only
thing he can. He gets stronger, quietly, and when the day comes he saves the people he can
reach first: his mother, his father, and his brother.

Arc one is the whole of Kingdom Come, both the fall of Skalitz and everything that follows all
the way to the end of the war. Hugo bends the story where he can and lets it run where he
should. Henry still has to walk his own road, find out whose son he really is, and earn his
own name. Only at the very end, when Henry is safe and home, does Hugo tell him the truth of
who and what he is, and then he leaves.

## The system

- Hugo speaks the word "System" and a panel appears that only he can see. It shows his current
  attributes and skills.
- System text is written plainly, inside square brackets, with the entire thing in bold. Keep it
  short. It is a tool to show progres of MC. Do not linger on it or let it crowd out the world.
  Example: `**[Strength: 6 -> 8.]**`
- The ceiling is this world's ceiling. There is no magic here. The best a man can be is a
  master of the sword, the bow, the horse, and his own nerves. Hugo can reach that peak. He
  cannot pass it.
- There is no shop and no free gifts. He grows by doing the thing, over and over, the honest
  way. What he owns he must find, earn, buy, or take.
- Hugo remembers the shape of this world from the game he once played. That memory is his real
  edge. He knows where useful things lie hidden near Skalitz and beyond, and he uses that
  knowledge to arm and feed his family before the war finds them.

## Tone & voice notes

- Grounded, muddy, and human. This is a hard century. People pray, drink, bleed, and die over
  small things. Keep the magic out. The only strange thing in the whole world is Hugo, and
  even he plays by the rules.
- Plain words. No fancy vocabulary. The speech should sound close to the people of the game:
  simple, a touch old, warm when it is warm and blunt when it is not. Curse when it fits.
- Point of view stays close on Hugo, third person. Tense moves the way normal telling does.
  Present tense for what is happening now, past tense when he looks back on something already
  done.
- Never use em dashes. Use commas, full stops, or start a new sentence.
- Let the quiet moments breathe. The bond between the brothers, the fear in a mother's face,
  the weight of knowing what is coming. Earn the big beats with the small ones.

## Constraints

- Hugo is Martin and Jane's true-born son. Henry is Jane's son by Radzig Kobyla, though nobody
  says so out loud until the end. That makes the boys half-brothers through their mother, and
  neither knows it at the start.
- Ages when Skalitz falls: Henry is nineteen, Hugo is eighteen. Hugo's memory woke at twelve.
- Hugo never hands out proof of the future. No lord would believe a smith's son. He works
  around that, not through it.
- Henry must still live his own story. He goes back for the survivors, loses his sword, and
  Hugo shadows him and helps save him alongside Theresa, holding on until Captain Bernard
  comes. Hugo allows Henry's road to happen; he does not walk it for him.
- The reveal of what Hugo truly is comes only at the very end of the arc, told to Henry alone,
  once Henry is home and safe at the mill with Theresa and their parents.
- Hugo and Rosa Ruthard share a fling, and both know it leads nowhere. For her it is a matter
  of station. For him it is that he will not be here forever. Keep it honest and adult, not
  cruel.
- This arc ends where the war ends. Future worlds are not this book's concern.
